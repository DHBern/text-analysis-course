# Bibliography

- Algee-Hewitt, Mark, Ryan Heuser, and Franco Moretti. "On Paragraphs: Scale, Themes, and Narrative Form." *Pamphlets of the Stanford Literary Lab* No. 10. Stanford, 2015. https://litlab.stanford.edu/LiteraryLabPamphlet10.pdf.
  - Uses topic modelling to show that themes are more concentrated in paragraphs than in arbitrary segments of text.

- Bamman, David, Kent K. Chang, Li Lucy, and Naitian Zhou. "On Classification with Large Language Models in Cultural Analytics." *Proceedings of the Computational Humanities Research Conference (CHR 2024)*: 494-527. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12029.
  - Compares LLMs with supervised models on ten tasks.

- Da, Nan Z. "The Computational Case against Computational Literary Studies." *Critical Inquiry* 45, no. 3 (2019): 601-639. https://doi.org/10.1086/702594.
  - A strong critique of the field.

- Gavin, Michael, Collin Jennings, Lauren Kersey, and Brad Pasanek. "Spaces of Meaning: Conceptual History, Vector Semantics, and Close Reading." In *Debates in the Digital Humanities 2019*, edited by Matthew K. Gold and Lauren F. Klein. University of Minnesota Press, 2019. https://dhdebates.gc.cuny.edu/read/4805e692-0823-4073-b431-5a684250a82d/section/4ce82b33-120f-423f-ba4c-40620913b305.
  - Combines vector semantics with close reading.

- Gius, Evelyn, and Janina Jacke. "The Hermeneutic Profit of Annotation: On Preventing and Fostering Disagreement in Literary Analysis." *International Journal of Humanities and Arts Computing* 11, no. 2 (2017): 233-254. https://doi.org/10.3366/ijhac.2017.0194.
  - Identifies four causes of disagreement between annotators.

- Hicke, Rebecca M. M., Yuri Bizzoni, Pascale Feldkamp, and Ross Deans Kristensen-McLachlan. "Says Who? Effective Zero-Shot Annotation of Focalization." *Anthology of Computers and the Humanities* 3 (2025): 738-754. https://doi.org/10.63744/xxqzxENxsh3b.
  - Zero-shot LLM annotation of an interpretive literary category.

- Rebora, Simone. *Computational Literary Studies*. Routledge, 2026. https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003631354.
  - A preview PDF is available at https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9781003631354/computational-literary-studies-simone-rebora.

- Sap, Maarten, Marcella Cindy Prasettio, Ari Holtzman, Hannah Rashkin, and Yejin Choi. "Connotation Frames of Power and Agency in Modern Films." *Proceedings of EMNLP 2017*: 2329-2334. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D17-1247.
  - A way to operationalise agency, from natural language processing.

- Underwood, Ted, David Bamman, and Sabrina Lee. "The Transformation of Gender in English-Language Fiction." *Journal of Cultural Analytics* 3, no. 2 (2018). https://doi.org/10.22148/16.019.
  - Gender in about 104,000 works of fiction.
